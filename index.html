Negative Pressure Wound Therapy (NPWT)

Negative Pressure Wound Therapy —
Made More Accessible.

Negative Pressure Wound Therapy (NPWT) is a wound-care technique that uses controlled negative pressure through a specialized dressing system to support wound management. It is used by healthcare professionals for selected chronic and difficult-to-heal wounds.

CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE

Why NPWT Matters

Negative Pressure Wound Therapy is an established therapeutic modality in hospital surgical and wound-care protocols globally.

Supports Wound Management

Maintains a protected, moist wound environment and creates mechanical micro-strain across the wound edges to support tissue granulation.

Helps Manage Wound Exudate

Provides active, closed-circuit evacuation of excess interstitial fluid and exudate into an airtight canister, reducing peri-wound maceration.

Used for Selected Complex Wounds

Indicated for selected challenging cases including chronic ulcers, dehisced surgical incisions, traumatic wounds, and partial-thickness burns.

Clinical Assessment & Monitoring

Requires appropriate assessment by trained healthcare professionals, regular dressing changes, and continuous pressure verification.

HEALTHCARE CHALLENGES

Making Advanced Wound Care More Accessible

Despite its clinical efficacy, traditional NPWT adoption remains severely constrained by systemic economic and operational bottlenecks.

High Cost of Existing NPWT Systems

High capital purchase prices, steep daily rental fees, and costly proprietary consumables make standard NPWT devices economically prohibitive for many healthcare institutions and patients.

Limited Accessibility

Advanced wound management technology is predominantly concentrated in metropolitan tertiary hospitals, leaving non-urban and community health centers without adequate NPWT resources.

Challenges for Smaller Hospitals & Clinics

Smaller nursing homes and community clinics face complex equipment operation, heavy maintenance overheads, and long supply chain lead times for specialized replacement parts.

Need for Compact & Practical Solutions

Clinicians require lightweight, bedside-ready, and straightforward devices that ensure safe pressure management without cumbersome user interfaces or excessive power demands.

OPERATIONAL ARCHITECTURE

How SAFE VAC Works

A clean conceptual framework connecting dressing interface, controlled sub-atmospheric suction, fluid management, and clinician monitoring.

STAGE 1

Wound Dressing

Specialized foam or gauze wound dressing placed into the debrided wound cavity and sealed with a transparent adhesive drape.

STAGE 2

SAFE VAC Device

Compact, accessibility-focused unit initiated by the clinician to generate controlled, regulated negative pressure.

STAGE 3

Controlled Negative Pressure

Regulated sub-atmospheric pressure (-70, -100, or -125 mmHg) applied uniformly across the dressing interface.

STAGE 4

Fluid Management

Exudate and infectious fluids safely aspirated into a designated containment canister equipped with hydrophobic filter.

STAGE 5

Healthcare Professional Monitoring

Periodic assessment by doctors and nurses to verify seal integrity, drainage volume, battery status, and wound progress.
